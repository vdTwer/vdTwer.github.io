Lately

I am Kolja. I build software that many people depend on. Right now that means the thirty thousand students of the University of Bonn, a flashcard service of my own and a master’s in computer science. I live between Cologne and Bonn.

Leading

The student parliament elected me head of IT for the AStA, the student government of the University of Bonn. I lead a team of five, two full-time staff and three students, and turn what people ask for, and what they need but don’t say, into software. Our minutes tool saves about seven hours of work a month. Now we are rebuilding the semester-ticket refund service, which pays six figures back to students every year.

I also sit on the advisory board of git.nrw, the shared GitLab of the universities in North Rhine-Westphalia, and speak there for about seven hundred thousand students. Lately we have been talking about how AI should come into it.

Building

AiNKI, because I love learning. I used Anki for years until it grew tedious on an iPhone, and I wasn’t going to pay €30 for the app. So I built my own, and while I was at it, I built more: it schedules every card with FSRS and speaks MCP, so an AI agent can write your cards and quiz you on them. It is written in Rust, self-hosted and open source.

Teaching

For three years I have taught programming at the Institute of Computer Science: exercise groups, reviews of more than two hundred student projects in C++ and Python, and five revision courses of up to one hundred students that I started on my own. These days I also teach agentic software engineering, the craft of building software with AI agents. In July I co-led a week-long algorithms workshop for gifted high-school students.

Studying
